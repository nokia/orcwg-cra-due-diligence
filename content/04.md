# What happens when due diligence fails

Due diligence fails when development practices of the open source component do not ensure
good code quality, the project does not have a way to report vulnerabilities, or the
community does not react to the reported vulnerabilities and release fixes. This means
that the usage of the open source component can compromise the cybersecurity of the product.

## Risk based analyzis

The CRA defines a risk based approch to due diligence. The cybersecurity risk of an opensource
component is not a standalone property. It depends on the appropriate level of cybersecurity
of the product that depends on the intended purpose and reasonably foreseeable use of the
product (ref [Guidance (157)][guidance-157]). It is also determined by what is the
functionality of the open source component and how it is used in the product.

This means that the appropiate level of due diligence can be applied based on the usage and
integration of the open source component to the product.

For example different level of due diligence is needed for a tamagotchi and a remote
controller device for home locks. And different level of due diligence is needed for an open
source component ensuring network security and an other component transforming simple text.

When the due diligence fails, determine the appropriate level of due diligence by analyzing
the intended purpose of the product, the functionality and the integration of the open source
component and apply an exception when it is appropriate. En exception means that even when due
diligence fails the manufacturer ensures that the integration of the open source component does
not compromise the cybersecurity of the product. In case of an exception always record the
decision with the recorded due diligence results.
